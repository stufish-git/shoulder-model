What may be causing it, best fit first

    Exercise rules: mild discomfort is fine, sharp pain is not. Stop any exercise that leaves the shoulder worse afterwards or the next day. Do a little, often. If it is no better after about six weeks, or it is getting worse, see a GP or physiotherapist. The videos were made by NHS physiotherapy services for their own patients and open on their sites.

    Get help fast: call 999 if shoulder or arm pain comes with chest tightness, breathlessness, sweating or feeling sick. Get seen the same day if the shoulder is hot, red and swollen with a fever, or looks out of shape after an injury. This guide suggests possibilities from where it hurts and your answers. It cannot examine you, so it is not a diagnosis, and the exercises are general ones, not a personal treatment plan.

    Medical names

    Top tendon
    Supraspinatus. Lifts the arm. Torn most often.
    Back tendon
    Infraspinatus. Turns the arm outward.
    Small back tendon
    Teres minor. Helps turn the arm outward.
    Front tendon
    Subscapularis. Turns the arm inward.
    Bony roof
    Acromion, part of the shoulder blade.
    Collarbone joint
    Acromioclavicular (AC) joint, where the collarbone meets the bony roof.
    Cushion sac
    Subacromial bursa. Lets the tendon slide under the roof.
